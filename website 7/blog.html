Section 32 depreciation: how financed machinery lowers your tax bill

Buying equipment on credit does not delay your tax benefit. As the owner under an equipment loan, you claim depreciation from the year the asset is put to use, and the interest in every EMI is a deductible business expense.

Read the full guide

Normal depreciation. Most plant and machinery is depreciated at 15% a year on the written-down value. If the asset is used for less than 180 days in the first year, half the rate applies.

Additional depreciation. Manufacturers buying new plant and machinery can claim a further 20% in the first year under Section 32(1)(iia). Companies that opt for the concessional tax rate under Section 115BAA cannot claim it, so model both options.

Leases are different. Under an operating lease the lessor owns the asset, so you deduct the full lease rental as an expense instead. Our EMI calculator estimates your first-year tax shield for each structure. Always confirm the final position with your chartered accountant.

Fixed vs floating rates on equipment loans: which saves more?

A floating rate looks cheaper on day one. We modelled 1,200 real loans to see when it actually is.

Read article

Across loans of 36 to 60 months, floating rates beat fixed rates only when rates fell in the first 18 months of the loan. For longer tenures the certainty of a fixed EMI usually wins for cash-flow planning.

If you choose floating, check the reset frequency and the spread over the benchmark, and confirm there is no penalty for switching to fixed later.

How to prepare a lender-ready loan file in one afternoon

Incomplete files are the number one cause of delays. Use this checklist to get everything right the first time.

Read article

Put your KYC, GST certificate, Udyam registration, six months of bank statements and the dealer quotation in a single folder, named clearly. Scanned PDFs should be legible and complete, with no missing pages.

Write a short note explaining any unusual entries in your bank statements, such as a large one-off receipt. Credit officers appreciate context and it prevents back-and-forth queries.
