FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about working with a broker.

What is the difference between a broker and an insurer?

An insurer carries the risk and pays claims. A broker like Kavach represents you, the buyer: we assess your risks, compare policies from many insurers, negotiate terms and help you through claims. An insurer's agent can only offer that one company's products.

Does using a broker cost more?

No. Our brokerage is paid by the insurer out of the premium, within limits set by the regulator, so you pay the same premium you would pay going direct. Because we compare the market, clients often end up paying less for better cover.

How do you help when I make a claim?

Call your advisor or log the claim in your client portal. Our claims team notifies the insurer, briefs the surveyor, tells you exactly which documents are needed and follows up until the settlement is paid. If an offer looks low, we challenge it on your behalf.

What is sum insured?

Sum insured is the maximum the insurer will pay for a loss. It should reflect the full reinstatement or replacement value of your building, machinery and stock. If it is too low, the insurer can apply "average" and reduce your claim proportionately, so we help you set it correctly.

How fast can cover start?

For standard property and liability risks, cover can often start within 24 to 48 hours of you accepting a quote and paying the premium. Large or high-hazard risks may need a site survey first, which usually takes a few working days.

What documents do I need for a quote?

Usually your company PAN and GST details, the address and construction details of each location, a breakdown of values (building, plant & machinery, stock), your current policy schedule if any, and a summary of claims from the last three years. Your advisor will share a simple checklist.
