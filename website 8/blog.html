Stock Moving Between Godowns? Why a Floating Policy Beats Fixed Locations

If your inventory shifts between warehouses every week, a policy that fixes a sum insured to each address leaves some sites over-insured and others dangerously short. Here is how floating and declaration policies fix that.

Read key takeaways
  • A floating policy covers one total sum insured across all listed locations, wherever the stock sits on the day of loss.
  • Declaration policies let you pay premium on your actual monthly stock, which suits seasonal businesses.
  • Insurers usually cap any one location at 25–40% of the total, so set that limit to your busiest godown.
  • Declare on time: a missed monthly declaration can be treated as nil stock for that location.

Sum Insured Too Low? How the Average Clause Quietly Cuts Your Claim

Under-insure your building by 30% and the insurer can cut every claim by 30%, even a small one. Here is how to set reinstatement values properly.

Key takeaways
  • Insure buildings at reinstatement cost, not market or book value.
  • Include plant, machinery and stock at current replacement value.
  • Add an escalation clause so values keep pace with construction inflation.
  • Review values every renewal, and after any expansion.

Monsoon Checklist for Warehouses and Factories

Flood and inundation claims spike every July. Ten minutes with this checklist before the rains can save weeks of downtime and a disputed claim.

Key takeaways
  • Raise stock at least 15 cm off the floor on pallets or racks.
  • Clear storm drains and roof gutters before June.
  • Photograph stock and premises before the rains for claim evidence.
  • Confirm your policy includes flood, storm and inundation (STFI).

What Insurers Expect From Your Locks, Safe and Alarm

Burglary claims are often reduced because of a missing grill or an unlocked safe. Know the security warranties in your policy before you need them.

Key takeaways
  • Burglary cover needs visible forcible entry; simple theft is usually excluded.
  • Cash must be in a locked safe meeting the policy specification.
  • Keep alarm maintenance records; insurers ask for them.
  • Inform the insurer if the premises will be unoccupied for over 30 days.

Public Liability for Shops: The Slip-and-Fall Risk

A customer slips on a wet floor and sues for medical costs and lost wages. Public liability cover pays the compensation and the lawyers.

Key takeaways
  • Choose a limit per accident and an annual aggregate that suits your footfall.
  • Product liability is separate if you manufacture or import goods.
  • Document floor cleaning and signage; it strengthens your defence.
  • Report any incident to us immediately, even if no claim is made yet.

The First 48 Hours After a Loss: A Claims Playbook

What you do in the two days after a fire or theft decides how smoothly your claim settles. Follow these steps in order.

Key takeaways
  • Make the site safe and call the fire brigade or police first.
  • Notify Kavach and the insurer within 24 hours; delays can void cover.
  • Do not discard damaged stock until the surveyor has inspected it.
  • Collect invoices, stock registers and photographs for the survey.

Do SME Boards Need Directors & Officers Cover?

Directors of private companies can be personally liable for regulatory breaches, unpaid dues and shareholder claims. D&O protects their personal assets.

Key takeaways
  • D&O pays defence costs and settlements for wrongful acts by directors.
  • Claims can come from regulators, lenders, employees or shareholders.
  • Cover is written on a claims-made basis, so keep it continuous.
  • Premiums for SMEs typically start from ₹40,000 for a ₹5 Cr limit.
